## 2. 英文版本（English Version）

**Subject: Appeal for Reconsideration of Manuscript Submission**

**Dear Frontiers Editorial Office,**

I am writing to submit our manuscript entitled "Measurement Reliability and Directional Disparity in Deep Learning Dietary Tracking Across Culinary Traditions: A Tri-Cohort Clinical Safety Assessment" to *Frontiers in Nutrition* (Section: Nutritional Methodology). Since a previous version of this manuscript was reviewed by your journal, we would like to provide several clarifications regarding the revised submission.

In the previous decision letter, it was stated that: “Issues related to compliance with research integrity policies, journal guidelines, or any other essential editorial criteria were identified in this manuscript that prevent further review or publication.” In response, our team conducted a thorough re-examination of all relevant requirements listed in the official Frontiers guidelines, including the policies on publication ethics and authorship responsibilities (https://www.frontiersin.org/guidelines/policies-and-publication-ethics#AuthorshipAuthorResponsibilities). We have also completed all necessary revisions accordingly.

Specifically, we have revised the manuscript to ensure that the total word count complies with the journal requirements. The main text now contains approximately 8,106 words, which is within the standard word limit for an Original Research article in *Frontiers in Nutrition*. We have also corrected the reference style to conform strictly to the standard Frontiers in Nutrition Vancouver numbered format. In addition, we have adjusted the placement and formatting of tables and figures, and all three high-resolution figures will be submitted separately through the submission system as required. We have also verified the manuscript similarity rate and AI-generated content rate using professional academic screening tools. According to our checks, the similarity rate is 12% and the AI-generated content rate is 2%, both of which reside comfortably within acceptable editorial thresholds. If required, we are fully prepared to provide the official verification certificates during the revision stage. Furthermore, we confirm that all authors have declared no potential conflicts of interest.

In addition, the revised manuscript is directly aligned with the scope of *Frontiers in Nutrition*, specifically the Nutritional Methodology specialty section. We have carefully reviewed the aims and submission criteria of this section. The journal specifically invites submissions concerning dietary intake assessment validity, measurement error evaluation, and the clinical application of digital health and machine learning in nutritional surveillance. This is highly consistent with the type and focus of our manuscript. Our study addresses a critical and under-explored clinical safety challenge in dietary computer vision: the systematic directional error that arises when deep regression models trained on North American institutional dining plates are deployed across non-Western culinary traditions. Using a standardized tri-cohort benchmark comprising 3,823 eating instances across Western (Nutrition5k), South Asian (FoodBD), and East Asian (ECUSTFD) dishes, we show that uncalibrated transfer to South Asian dining induces severe carbohydrate underestimation (-18.18 g, p = 8.64 * 10^-206), reducing American Diabetes Association insulin dosing compliance to 49.5%. We further demonstrate via orthogonal error decomposition that unobserved ingredient densities within opaque preparations explain over 75% of cross-dataset variance, and that a localized 50-meal recalibration restores clinical compliance to 63.9%. The manuscript follows STROBE-nut and TRIPOD-AI reporting standards and provides an open-source repository (https://github.com/Hajimi-Sudo/cross-cultural-diet-ai-bias-audit).

For these reasons, we believe that our manuscript fully complies with the basic submission guidelines of Frontiers and is directly relevant to the theme of *Frontiers in Nutrition*. We respectfully request an opportunity for editorial reconsideration, as we believe that this manuscript offers solid scientific value, clinical safety implications, and algorithmic equity considerations for global nutrition. In light of the potential contribution of this study to the field, we kindly ask the journal to reconsider its decision and allow the manuscript to proceed to editorial assignment and peer review.

We fully understand that the manuscript may still be rejected after peer review, and we would accept such an outcome. However, we sincerely hope that our manuscript can receive a fair and specialized evaluation by Frontiers reviewers. We highly value the rigorous standards upheld by Frontiers journals and believe that this work may provide meaningful value to the scientific community.
